Dragon Kisser

Game Environment · Real-Time · Student Capstone

This is a student capstone project successfully published on Steam. I worked on this project as an Environment Artist Lead who actively communicated with environment, art, and programming teams to balance visual quality with technical efficiency. I collaborated on creating a medieval Spanish village surrounding the arena and was solely responsible for the rhythm-minigame background environment. I used Maya and ZBrush for modeling, Substance Painter and Substance Designer for texturing, Unreal Engine 5 as the game engine, and Perforce for version control.

View on Steam ↗

The environment team's goal was to create an immersive atmosphere without distracting players from the core gameplay, while maintaining engaging visual elements. We arranged houses with different heights and roof shapes to create an irregular skyline behind the arena. We also placed small props to populate the environment and help players recognize the playable boundary.

For the rhythm-minigame environment, I arranged the background to guide players' attention toward the central stage by creating a leading line with props such as signs and lanterns. I also placed candles and rose petals to create a romantic atmosphere. I modeled the central stage asset by interpreting concept art from our Art Director.

To populate the environment and give the architecture character, I modeled decorative props, such as lanterns, planters, and chimneys. Because these props were instanced throughout the environment, I kept their polygon counts low while preserving their overall shapes.

I also set up the house Blueprints so that we can easily customize houses with exposed parameters for selecting prop models.

I created brick and stucco texture sets using Substance Designer for the mask-driven material used on house walls. I also made a trim sheet for the remaining architectural components. We ended up using two materials to shade all the house assets: the wall-blending material and the material using the trim sheet.

I made one master material for houses that uses in-engine masking, which enables us to paint a mask while in Mesh Paint Mode. By doing so, brick and stucco materials could blend naturally in the engine and we could change the stucco coverage over bricks inside Unreal instead of authoring a new texture variation for each house in Substance Painter.

I set up master materials that are reusable for all small props. I showed environment artists how to assign their textures in the material instance's parameter panel and create simple variations by adjusting parameters.
